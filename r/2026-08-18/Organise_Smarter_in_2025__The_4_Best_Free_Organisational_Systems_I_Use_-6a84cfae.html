Hands On Katie proposes a four-quadrant approach to home organisation: small and large items, each stored horizontally or vertically. Her recommended stack is Gridfinity for small drawer items, NeoGrid for large drawers, French cleats for heavy or large wall storage, and openGrid for smaller vertical storage. Her through-line is interoperability, permissive community-oriented design, simple workflows, and using non-printed materials where they improve the result.

What this video offers

Choose systems that connect across ecosystems rather than locking users into incompatible parts or restrictive licences.

Treat simplicity as a design test: a system should be quickly understandable while still supporting extensive customisation.

Gridfinity has the strongest ecosystem for small horizontal storage; its bins, customisers, labels, lids, click grids, and portable cases add capability without changing the core model.

NeoGrid 2.0 is her large-drawer choice, particularly because it can work alongside Gridfinity and now supports openGrid integration through matching dimensions and adapters.

For heavy or large vertical storage, she favours French cleats over lighter-duty printed wall systems.

She moved from Multiboard to openGrid for small vertical items, citing openGrid's interoperability, adapters, lower material use, faster printing, and community-oriented development.

The next challenge is aesthetics: blend functional printed organisers with warmer materials, lighting, and designs suited to living spaces.
